# The setup interview, and bootstrapping the memory

The goal is a genuinely good first slate after about eight minutes of questions, not a complete profile. Everything you don't ask now, the weekly runs learn from their votes and notes. So ask about the few things that are expensive to get wrong, like diets, budgets, key dates and hard no's, and default the rest.

## 1. How to ask

- **Read the room first.** If their opening message already answers something ("we're in {city}, one of us is vegan, we love live music"), don't ask it again. Confirm briefly and move on.
- Ask in **rounds of up to four questions**. If the environment has a structured question tool (multiple choice plus free text), use it. Otherwise use a numbered list with lettered options, and tell them a reply like `1b 2a 3: Fridays and Sunday daytime` is fine.
- **Every question gets suggested answers.** Put the most common or sensible one first and mark it *(suggested)*. People answer faster when they can react instead of invent.
- **"Skip" is always fine.** Use the default and record it in `profile.md` as `(default, unconfirmed)`, so later runs know it's still open.
- **Ask about both people.** Several questions, diets especially, have two answers. Ask for each person by name.
- **Keep it light.** One short line of framing per round is plenty.

## 2. Round 1: the basics

1. **What should the page call you two?** These are the names on the voting buttons.
   *First names (suggested) · Nicknames · Initials*
2. **Where's home base?** Neighborhood and city, or a nearby landmark. That's enough for walking and driving times. Don't ask for a street address.
3. **How far will you go for a normal date night?**
   *Walking or transit only · Up to ~30 min (suggested) · Up to an hour · Happy to do day trips too*
4. **When do dates usually happen?** Pick all that apply, plus a dinner time.
   *Friday evening · Saturday daytime · Saturday evening · Sunday daytime · Weeknights are fine for things with no fixed date*
   *Dinner: early (6–7) · around 7:30 (suggested) · late (8:30+)*

## 3. Round 2: what you must never get wrong

Frame it: *"A few things that, if I get them wrong, make the suggestions useless."* Food is usually the category a planner suggests most, so a diet discovered weeks in quietly spoils every food suggestion before it.

5. **Diet, for each of you.** Ask person by person.
   *No restrictions · Vegetarian · Vegan · Pescatarian · Halal · Kosher · Gluten-free · Allergy (which?) · No alcohol*
6. **Budget per person.** Ask for a normal date and for a special occasion, in local currency.
   *Normal: under 25 · 25–60 (suggested) · 60–120 · 120+ · Occasion: up to 2× normal (suggested) · no ceiling*
7. **Dates to plan around.** Anniversary, both birthdays, anything else you celebrate. How early should the planner start suggesting things?
   *3 weeks ahead (suggested) · 6 weeks · just the week before*
8. **What shapes your weekends, and what's off the table?**
   - Standing commitments, like a regular family lunch, a game you always watch, religious observance, kids or pets, or on-call work.
   - Hard no's, like crowds, heights, horror, clubs, long drives, or anything else.

## 4. Round 3: taste

9. **Tell me about two or three of your best dates.** What made them good? This is the most useful answer in the whole interview, because it seeds the "tried" pool and the first tag weights.
10. **Quick "this or that".** One pick per pair, or "both".
    *Doing something ↔ watching something · Somewhere new ↔ an old favorite · Small room ↔ big event · Planned ahead ↔ decided on the day · Active ↔ chilled out*
11. **What's in?** Multi-select, then ask whether anything specific is already on their wishlist.
    *Restaurants · Cocktail or wine bars · Live music · Comedy · Theatre, dance or classical · Film · Classes and workshops · Outdoors and nature · Active (hikes, climbing, bikes, water) · Games (bowling, trivia, board games, escape rooms) · Museums and exhibitions · Markets and festivals · Spa and wellness · Day trips · Night sky and nature events · Live sports*
    Also ask about cuisines, loved and avoided.
12. **Who or what would you book weeks ahead for?** Artists, comedians, teams, a festival, a theatre company. If they want to share a music-library export or a "top artists" list, take it, but treat it as evidence of taste, not appetite (see `../weekly-run/references/learning.md`).
    Also ask: **which booking apps do you actually use** for tables and for tickets? That way the cards point to the right place.

## 5. Defaults to confirm

Offer these as one block that they can accept with a single "yes" or change line by line:

- **Weekly run:** *Wednesdays around 7am, {their time zone}.* That leaves time to vote and book before the weekend.
- **Options per week:** *4* (3 and 5 also work).
- **Adventurousness:** *balanced, adapting to what you pick* (or "mostly new things", or "mostly favorites").
- **Photos on the page:** *none*, or the name of a Google Drive folder of their photos to show at the top.
- **A weekly email with the link:** *off*, or on to both addresses. Only offer this if a mail connector is available.
- **Conversation nudge:** *off*. It's an occasional one-line prompt at the bottom of the page, at most every other week.

## 6. Optional head start from email and calendar

If a mail or calendar connector is available, offer it. Ask permission first, and explain what you'll keep:

> "Want me to look back through your email and calendar for past restaurant bookings and tickets? I'd keep only a summary (places, rough dates, how often, price level) to learn what you already like. No order numbers or seat details."

If they agree:
- Search a year or two of reservation and ticket confirmations, plus calendar events that look like outings.
- Apply the evidence-worth table in `../weekly-run/references/learning.md`. Repeat visits are the strongest signal. Takeout and delivery count for a quarter. Weekday lunches, groups of six or more, and work events count for nothing.
- Derive repeat favorites for the tried pool, cuisines, typical days and times, real price level, how far ahead they book, and which platforms they use.
- Record the blind spot in `profile.md`: **this is one account's view.** Bookings made from any other account won't show up, so whole categories may be undercounted. Treat "no evidence" there as *unknown*, not *untried*.

## 7. Bootstrapping the files

Fill these from the answers. Templates are in `../templates/`, and each file's structure is described in `../weekly-run/references/memory-files.md`.

**`profile.md`:** everything from rounds 1–3 and the defaults. Mark any skipped answers `(default, unconfirmed)`. Fill in Delivery and schedule, and put any dial they changed into House rules.

**`sources.md`:** research their city now. Don't rely on memory, because listings sites come and go. Find and verify:
- the main local events listings, and coverage of new restaurant openings
- an alternative or culture-focused events listing
- venue calendars across sizes: small clubs, mid-size theatres, arenas
- symphony, theatre and dance seasons
- festival and street-fair calendars, and park, garden and nature-event calendars
- niche promoters or communities that match their tastes and rarely reach mainstream listings, such as a language community's concert promoter, a genre-specific venue, or a climbing gym's events page
- a "within about three hours" list of towns, for day trips and for tours that skip their city
- a reliable local forecast and air-quality source

Mark the two or three sources they already check. The planner's value is finding what those miss.

**`radar.md`:** performers, series and seasons from question 12, with a line on why each one matters to them.

**`backlog.md`:** start with their own wishlist. Then research 10–15 ideas in their city that fit their answers, check each one exists and roughly what it costs, tag it, and run a quick **suggestion round**:

> "Here are 12 ideas for {city}. For each, reply keep, drop, or 'done it, loved it'."

- Kept ideas go in as `open`.
- "Done it, loved it" goes into the tried pool with item score +3, and +1 on each of its tags.
- Dropped ideas go under Dropped, with the reason if they gave one.

This is already real feedback, and it sharpens the first slate a lot.

**`weights.md`:** seed modestly, because stated preferences are weaker than observed ones.
- A clear pick in a this-or-that pair: +1.0 on that side's tag, −0.5 on the opposite.
- A category they marked "in": +0.5.
- Something named in a best date: +1.0 on each of its tags.
- A hard no is **not** a weight. It goes in `profile.md` as a constraint, so it filters instead of just scoring low.
- Anything not mentioned stays at 0 and goes under "Untested gaps", so it gets the gap bonus and is tried eventually.

Log each seed in the change log with where it came from ("setup: best date #2").

**`history.md`:** just the header. The first slate adds the first block.

Finally, show a compact summary of what you understood, around ten lines covering names, area, days, diets, budget, key dates, and top loves and no's. Ask them to correct anything wrong, and fix it immediately.
